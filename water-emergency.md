# What to Do If You See Water in Your Unit

Water incidents are the most time-sensitive issues in a condominium. Fast action protects your Unit, your neighbors, and Association Property. Delayed action can shift liability and cost onto you. This is what to do.

## Emergency contacts

| Situation | Who to call |
|---|---|
| Life safety, fire, or active fire sprinkler discharge | **911** first, then management |
| Water main or potable (drinking) water leak | **Irvine Ranch Water District (IRWD) 24-hour line: [confirm number]** |
| Tribeca landscape or irrigation sprinkler leak | **Earthco 24-hour line: (949) 204-6085** |
| Central Park West (master association) irrigation leak | **Contact Central Park West (master association): [confirm contact]** |
| Report any water incident to management | **Management 24-hour line: [confirm number]** |

Note the exact location before you call so the right crew is sent.

## If you see water, do these 5 things right now

1. Shut off the water at the nearest valve if you can do so safely.
2. Move belongings out of the area. Contain the spread with towels.
3. Take photos and video before you clean up.
4. Notify management in writing within 24 hours. Written notice is required.
5. If water is flowing to a neighbor or common area, notify them right away.

## Reporting

Contact management through your homeowner portal. Written notice within 24 hours of discovery is a condition of any Association coverage under the Residential Handbook. Written notice is the record. A phone call alone does not count.

## When to call 911 first

Active fire sprinkler discharge. Water touching electrical panels or outlets. Structural intrusion getting rapidly worse. Any life-safety concern. Call 911, then notify management.

Do not attempt to shut off or manipulate a fire sprinkler head yourself.

## Outdoor and irrigation leaks: who to call

Water outside a Unit is handled differently depending on the source. Identify the exact location first, then use this guide.

1. **Water main or potable water leak** (a drinking-water line, a meter, or a street main): this is the water district. Call the **Irvine Ranch Water District (IRWD) 24-hour line: [confirm number]**.
2. **Landscape or irrigation sprinkler leak** (a sprinkler head, drip line, or valve in a planter, lawn, or common area): first confirm whose line it is.
   - If it is a **Tribeca** irrigation line, call **Earthco 24-hour line: (949) 204-6085**. Be specific about the location so the right crew is dispatched.
   - If it is a **Central Park West (master association)** irrigation line and not a Tribeca line, contact **Central Park West (the master association): [confirm contact]**, not Earthco.
3. **If you are unsure of the source or location,** report it to management right away rather than guessing.

Being specific about the location matters. Tribeca and Central Park West maintain different irrigation lines, and the wrong crew cannot fix the wrong system.

## Locate your Unit shutoff before you need it

Every Unit has a shutoff valve, typically in a wall panel, laundry closet, or utility box within or adjacent to the Unit. Find yours today. Show your household. Two minutes now saves hours later.

## The 1 preventive habit that matters

Supply lines and hoses to washing machines, dishwashers, toilets, and faucets fail with age. Inspect annually. Replace braided stainless supply lines every 5 to 7 years. This prevents most of the incidents we see.

More detail below for those who want it.

## Where is the water coming from

Coordinate with neighbors. Water travels through walls, ceilings, and floors and does not always originate where it appears.

- **From your ceiling:** likely the Unit above or a shared pipe. Contact your upstairs neighbor and management.
- **From your walls:** possibly a shared riser or a neighboring Unit. Contact management and your adjacent neighbor.
- **From a fixture in your Unit** (sink, toilet, tub, shower, dishwasher, washing machine, water heater): originates in your Unit. Shut off the fixture valve or Unit shutoff. Engage a licensed plumber.
- **From a fire sprinkler head:** call 911 first if actively discharging, then management.
- **From an exterior source** (roof, window frame, balcony deck, exterior wall): Association Property. Report to management with photos.
- **Uncertain:** report anyway. Do not delay while you try to diagnose it.

## Who is responsible (CC&Rs Exhibit C)

**Owner:** in-Unit plumbing fixtures, supply lines and hoses, water heater, water pressure regulator, angle stops and Unit shutoffs, drain lines through laterals to the main, sprinkler heads solely serving the Unit, HVAC condensate lines.

**Association:** pipes and fixtures serving more than 1 Unit or Association Property, sprinkler system components serving more than 1 Unit, roof, exterior walls, foundation, common area risers and mains.

**Default rule when Exhibit C is silent:** exclusively serves 1 Unit means Owner. Serves more than 1 Unit or Association Property means Association.

The CC&Rs control if there is any conflict with this summary.

## Owner liability for leaks

Directly from CC&Rs Exhibit C:

> Owners are responsible for damage to the Unit, to other Units and to Neighborhood Association Property to extent caused by Owner or tenant failure to prevent or stop leaks or overflows in the Unit.

From the Residential Handbook:

> The Neighborhood Association will only be responsible for water damage if the moisture intrusion or leak is reported to the Neighborhood Association, in writing, within 24 hours of the Owner's discovery of the moisture intrusion leak. If an Owner allows water damage or moisture to remain in the Unit for longer than 24 hours, the Neighborhood Association cannot be held responsible for resulting damage, including discoloration, mold or mildew that develops.

Two implications. Prompt written notice is a condition of Association coverage. Ignoring a slow leak converts an Association issue into an Owner issue.

## Vendor rules

Owners hiring plumbers for in-Unit work are encouraged to use qualified plumbers. Any work outside the Unit airspace, meaning inside wall cavities, on risers, on common piping, or on the fire sprinkler system, must be performed by a plumber approved by the Association. Contact management before scheduling that work.

Thank you for helping keep the community protected.
