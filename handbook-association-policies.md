# Association Policies and Other Information

This page reproduces the enforcement of governing documents, the Neighborhood Association Guidelines Basics, the Code of Conduct, and the assessments and foreclosure notice.

Source: Neighborhood Guidelines (Residential Handbook), Tribeca at Central Park West Association, adopted by the Board July 24, 2018.

---

## Enforcement of Governing Documents

If a member believes that a violation of the Governing Documents has occurred, then he/she should complete a Violation Report Form (attached) and submit it to the Manager. No Member complaint can be acted upon unless there is supporting documentation, i.e., the written complaint, witness statements, dated photos.

Owners are responsible for their own violations and the violations of their family members, guests, invitees and tenants.

The Neighborhood Association's Enforcement Policy and Fine Schedule are also attached to these Guidelines. Please review this Policy carefully.

## Neighborhood Association Guidelines Basics

**1.** All Owners, residents, tenants, their guests, visitors and other invitees are presumed to conduct themselves in a respectful and courteous manner with due consideration for each other as well as Neighborhood Association employees, The Manager Staff, vendors and their respective employees (herein collectively referred to as the ("staff").

**2.** The use of obscene gestures, obscenities, verbal or physical threats or attacks directed at any Owner, resident, tenant, guest, visitor, invitee, or to Community Staff will not be tolerated. Action by any person within Tribeca on any Neighborhood Association Property (including, but not limited to, the Recreational Facilities) which may be dangerous or create a health or safety concern, a hostile environment, turmoil, disruption or disturbance among Owners, residents or others is not permitted.

**3.** Residents are responsible for any damage to the Neighborhood Association Property, Recreational Facilities and/or non-compliance with the Governing Documents caused by or resulting from Owner's own actions or the actions of family members, tenants, guests, service vendors, contractors and other invitees, consistent with the above paragraph.

**4.** Community Staff are to be treated in a courteous and considerate manner at all times. Community Staff shall not be reprimanded or harassed in any way by an Owner, resident, tenant, guest or other invitee. All complaints regarding service rendered by any Community Staff member must be made to the Manager for appropriate handling.

**5.** The Neighborhood Association considers non-compliance with the Code of Conduct by Owners or the Owner's tenants, residents, guests, invitees, agents and/or employees serious and will subject the responsible Owner to disciplinary action in accordance with the Neighborhood Association's Compliance Procedure.

**6.** In the event that an Owner fails to comply with the Code of Conduct, Neighborhood Association Staff may, in their discretion, ask the Owner or guest to vacate the premises and take whatever action is required to cause this action to occur.

**7.** Know that the intent of the Neighborhood Association is to operate, manage and maintain the community for the enjoyment of all.

**8.** Enjoy your ownership at the Tribeca Neighborhood.

**9.** For further information on Tribeca at Central Park West Neighborhood Association and the community, contact the Manager.

## Code of Conduct

All Owners, family members, tenants, their guests, visitors and other invitees are presumed to conduct themselves in a respectful and courteous manner with due consideration for each other as well as Association employees, The Manager Staff, vendors and their respective employees (herein collectively referred to as the "Association Staff").

The use of obscene gestures, obscenities, verbal or physical threats or attacks directed at any Owner, resident, tenant, guest, visitor, invitee, or to Association Staff will not be tolerated. Action by any person within the Neighborhood on any Neighborhood Association Property which may be dangerous or create a health or safety concern, a hostile environment, turmoil, disruption or disturbance among Owners, residents or others is not permitted.

The Owner is responsible for any damage to the Neighborhood Association Property and/or non-compliance with the Governing Documents caused by or resulting from Owner's own actions or the actions of family members, tenants, guests, service vendors, contractors and other invitees, consistent with the above paragraph.

Association Staff are to be treated in a courteous and considerate manner at all times. Association Staff shall not be reprimanded or harassed in any way by an Owner, resident, tenant, guest or other invitee. All complaints regarding service rendered by any Association Staff member must be made to the Association's General Manager or Board of Directors for appropriate handling.

The Association considers non-compliance with this Conduct Code by Owners or the Owner's tenants, residents, guests, invitees, agents and/or employees serious and will subject the responsible Owner to disciplinary action in accordance with the Association's Compliance Procedure.

In the event that an Owner fails to comply with this Conduct Code, Association Staff may, in their discretion, ask the Owner or guest to leave the premises and take whatever action is required to cause this action to occur.

## Assessments and Foreclosure

This notice outlines some of the rights and responsibilities of owners of property in common interest developments and the Neighborhood Associations that manage them. Please refer to the sections of the Civil Code indicated for further information. A portion of the information in this notice applies only to liens recorded on or after January 1, 2003. You may wish to consult a lawyer if you dispute an assessment.

### Assessments and Non-Judicial Foreclosure

The failure to pay Neighborhood Association assessments may result in the loss of an owner's property without court action, often referred to as non-judicial foreclosure. When using non-judicial foreclosure, the Neighborhood Association records a lien on the owner's property. The owner's property may be sold to satisfy the lien if the lien is not paid. Assessments become delinquent 15 days after they are due, unless the governing documents of the Neighborhood Association provide for a longer time (Section 1366 and 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

In a non-judicial foreclosure, the Neighborhood Association may recover assessments, reasonable costs of collection, reasonable attorney's fees, late charges, and interest. The Neighborhood Association may not use non-judicial foreclosure to collect fines or penalties, except for costs to repair Neighborhood Association Property damaged by a member or a members guests, if the governing documents provide for this. (Sections 1366 and 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

The Neighborhood Association must comply with the requirements of Section 1367.1 of the Civil Code when collecting delinquent assessments. If the Neighborhood Association fails to follow these requirements, it may not record a lien on the owner's property until it has satisfied those requirements. Any additional costs that result from satisfying the requirements are the responsibility of the Neighborhood Association. (Section 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

At least 30 days prior to recording a lien on an owner's separate interest, the Neighborhood Association must provide the owner of record with certain documents by certified mail. Among these documents, the Neighborhood Association must send a description of its collection and lien enforcement procedures and the method of calculating the amount. It must also provide an itemized statement of the charges owed by the owner. An owner has a right to review the Neighborhood Association's records to verify the debt. (Section 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

If a lien is recorded against an owner's property in error, the person who recorded the lien is required to record a lien release within 21 days, and to provide an owner certain documents in this regard. (Section 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

The collection practices of the Neighborhood Association may be governed by state and federal laws regarding fair debt collection. Penalties can be imposed for debt collection practices that violate these laws.

### Payments

When an owner makes a payment, he or she may request a receipt and the Neighborhood Association is required to provide it. On the receipt, the Neighborhood Association must indicate the date of payment and the person who received it. The Neighborhood Association must inform owners of a mailing address for overnight payments (Sections 1367.1 and 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

An owner may, but is not obligated to, pay under protest any disputed charge or sum levied by the Neighborhood Association, including, but not limited to, an assessment, fine, penalty, late fee, collection cost, or monetary penalty imposed as a disciplinary measure, and by so doing, specifically reserve the right to contest the disputed charge or sum in court or otherwise.

An owner may dispute an assessment debt by submitting a written request for dispute resolution to the Neighborhood Association as set forth in Article 5 (commencing with Section 1368.810) of Chapter 4 of Title 6 of Division of the Civil Code. In addition, a Neighborhood Association may not initiate a foreclosure without participating in alternative dispute resolution with a neutral third party as set forth in Article 2 (commencing with Section 1369.510) of Chapter 7 of Title 6 of Division 2 of the Civil Code, if so requested by the owner. Binding arbitration shall not be available if the Neighborhood Association intends to initiate a judicial foreclosure.

An owner is not liable for charges, interest, and costs of collection, if it is established that the assessment was paid properly on time. (Section 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

### Meetings and Payment Plans

An owner of a separate interest that is not a time-share may request the Neighborhood Association to consider a payment plan to satisfy a delinquent assessment. The Neighborhood Association must inform owners of the standards for payment plans, if any exist. (Section 1367.1 of the Civil Code)

The board of directors must meet with an owner who makes a proper written request for a meeting to discuss a payment plan when the owner has received a notice of a delinquent assessment. These payment plans must conform with the payment plan standards of the Neighborhood Association, if they exist. (Section 1367.1 of the Civil Code).
