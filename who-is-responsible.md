# Who Is Responsible? Owner vs Association

A plain-language guide to whether you or the Association is responsible for a repair or maintenance issue. This is a convenience summary. Every assignment below is grounded in the recorded CC&Rs, and the CC&Rs control if anything here differs from them.

Source: Neighborhood Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions and Reservation of Easements (CC&Rs) for Tribeca at Central Park West, recorded August 14, 2017 as Instrument 2017000341565, Orange County Official Records. Article 2, Section 2.1 (Repair and Maintenance) and Exhibit C (Specific Maintenance Obligations).

## How to figure out who is responsible

1. **Identify the component, not the symptom.** "Water on my ceiling" is a symptom. The responsible item is a pipe, a fixture, a roof, or a neighbor's shower. Narrow to the actual component first.
2. **Check the maintenance matrix (Exhibit C).** Exhibit C lists specific components and assigns each to the Owner or the Association. See the full [Maintenance Obligations](maintenance-obligations.html) and [CC&Rs Article 2](ccrs-article-2.html).
3. **Apply the default rule.** Under CC&Rs Section 2.1.3(c), the Neighborhood Association is "responsible for all maintenance, repair and replacement not provided by ... the Owners, pursuant to Section 2.1.2, Exhibit C."
4. **When it is not clearly one or the other, do not guess.** Under CC&Rs Section 2.1.4, the Board determines a disputed maintenance obligation and that decision is binding. Contact management before acting.

<div class="tb-callout"><strong>The default rule.</strong> Serves only your Unit, it is the Owner (CC&amp;Rs Section 2.1.2 and Exhibit C). Serves more than one Unit or common area, it is the Association (CC&amp;Rs Section 2.1.3(c)). Exhibit C controls for any component it lists.</div>

## Common pitfalls to watch for

- **Symptom vs source.** A leak showing on your ceiling is often not your problem. Trace to the component before assigning responsibility (Exhibit C).
- **"If accessible" windows.** Under Exhibit C you clean glass you can safely reach. The Association handles upper-story exterior glass through the scheduled washing program. Balcony sliders are always Owner because they are accessible from the balcony.
- **Angle stops and Unit shutoffs.** Always Owner under Exhibit C. They sit inside your Unit airspace and serve only your Unit.
- **Fire sprinklers.** Heads solely serving your Unit are Owner (Exhibit C). Everything upstream, the pipes, valves, and gauges, is Association (CC&Rs Section 2.1.3(c)). Never manipulate a sprinkler head.
- **Damage you cause.** Under CC&Rs Section 2.1.6, each Owner is liable to the Association for damage to Association Property caused by the negligence or willful act of the Owner or the Owner's family, tenants, or guests, and the Association may levy a Special Assessment to recover the cost.
- **24-hour written notice on water.** Under the Residential Handbook, the Association is only responsible for water damage reported in writing within 24 hours of your discovery. Verbal reports do not count. See [What to Do If You See Water](water-emergency.html).

## If you believe the Association is responsible

The default is that in-unit issues and owner-maintained components are the Owner's responsibility (Exhibit C and CC&Rs Section 2.1.3). If you believe the source is a component the Association maintains, such as a pipe serving more than one Unit, a structural element, or a common-area component, provide the following so management can evaluate your report:

- A written description of the issue, with dates and the location within your Unit
- Photos and video of the condition
- Why you believe the source is an Association-maintained component, identifying the specific component if you can
- Confirmation that you have ruled out obvious in-unit sources, such as appliances, drains, plumbing fixtures, cabinetry, food waste, pets, and personal belongings

Submit this from the Owner of record. A tenant or representative may participate only with the Owner's written authorization. Management compares the report against Exhibit C and Section 2.1.3. If it identifies an Association-maintained component, the Association arranges verification and, if confirmed, engages its own vendor. If the source is in your Unit or an owner-maintained component, the cost remains with the Owner.

For emergencies such as active flooding, gas, sparking electrical, sewage backup, or structural failure, do not wait. See [What to Do If You See Water](water-emergency.html) and call 911 for any life-safety risk.

## When to contact management first

Under CC&Rs Section 2.1.4, if a maintenance obligation is disputed, the Board determines who is responsible and that decision is binding. Contact management before acting when Exhibit C is unclear, the water source is unclear or spans multiple Units, or the work touches shared components or the sprinkler system.
