# Article 2 — Condominium and Use Restrictions

This article sets out the condominium use restrictions that apply to Owners and residents, covering maintenance, balconies, leasing, nuisances, signs, parking, animals, antennas, trash, installations and building systems.

Source: Neighborhood Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions and Reservation of Easements for Tribeca at Central Park West, recorded August 14, 2017 as Instrument 2017000341565, Orange County Official Records. Article 2, pages 13–30.

---

The Neighborhood shall be held, used and enjoyed subject to the following restrictions and subject to the exemptions of Declarant set forth in the Governing Documents. To the extent that any of the following restrictions in this Article 2 conflict with the Master Declaration, then the more restrictive provision shall control, subject to applicable law.

## 2.1 Repair and Maintenance

### 2.1.1 Maintenance Standards

The Neighborhood Association shall maintain everything it is obligated to maintain in a clean, sanitary and attractive condition reasonably consistent with the level of maintenance reflected in the most current Budget, and in conformance with all applicable Maintenance Guidelines and the provisions of the Master Declaration. Unless specifically provided in any Maintenance Guidelines or in the Master Declaration, the Board shall determine, in its sole discretion, the level and frequency of maintenance of the Neighborhood Association Property and Improvements thereon. Each Owner shall maintain everything the Owner is obligated to maintain in a clean, sanitary and attractive condition and in conformance with any Maintenance Recommendations.

### 2.1.2 By Owners

#### (a) In General

Owners repair, maintenance and replacement responsibilities with are set forth on Exhibit C attached hereto. The repair, maintenance and replacement responsibilities set forth on Exhibit C shall apply to the Owners in each Phase unless the Declarant records a Notice of Addition setting forth different repair, maintenance and replacement responsibilities with respect to Improvements located in a particular Phase. Owners shall perform all required maintenance, repair and replacement in accordance with the Governing Documents. Unless otherwise specified in the Governing Documents, (i) the Owner's maintenance obligation includes the obligation to keep the items Owner is obligated to maintain in a clean and sanitary condition, free of debris and reasonably protected from damage, and (ii) each Owner shall bear all costs and expenses of the maintenance, repair and replacement that the Owner is required to perform pursuant to the Neighborhood Declaration. Each Owner shall pay when due all charges for any utility service which is separately metered to the Owner's Unit. If the Neighborhood Association does not adopt an inspection and prevention program with regard to wood-destroying pests and other organisms pursuant to Section 2.1.3(d), then such a program is the responsibility of each Owner.

#### (b) MERV Filters

The Units in the Neighborhood contain Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value 14 ("MERV 14") filters within the intake of the Unit's ventilation system. Each Owner of a Unit with a MERV 14 filter shall maintain the MERV 14 filter at all times in accordance with the manufacturer's recommendations, any Maintenance Guidelines provided by Declarant, and any Maintenance Guidelines provided by the Neighborhood Association.

### 2.1.3 By Neighborhood Association

#### (a) Commencement of Obligations

The Neighborhood Association's obligation to maintain Neighborhood Association Property in any Phase commences on the date Annual Assessments commence on Condominiums in the Phase that includes the Neighborhood Association Property. Until commencement of Annual Assessments on Condominiums in any Phase in which a Condominium is located, Declarant shall maintain the Neighborhood Association Property in such Phase. The Neighborhood Association's obligation to maintain the Neighborhood Association Property in a Phase comprised solely of Neighborhood Association Property shall commence on conveyance of such Neighborhood Association Property to the Neighborhood Association.

#### (b) Maintenance Standards

Subject to Articles 9 and 10, the Neighborhood Association shall maintain the Neighborhood Association Property and Improvements thereon or shall contract to assure the Neighborhood Association Property and Improvements thereon are maintained in a clean, sanitary and attractive condition reasonably consistent with the level of maintenance reflected in the most current Budget. However, the Neighborhood Association is not responsible for performing any maintenance, repair or replacement which is made the Owner's responsibility pursuant to Section 2.1.2 or Exhibit C, or pursuant to any Owner maintenance, repair or replacement responsibilities set forth in a Notice of Addition for a particular Phase. The Board shall determine, in its sole discretion, the level and frequency of maintenance of the Neighborhood Association Property and Improvements thereon.

#### (c) Maintenance Items

The Neighborhood Association shall be responsible for all maintenance, repair and replacement not provided by either the Owners, pursuant to Section 2.1.2, Exhibit C, and pursuant to any Owner maintenance, repair or replacement responsibilities set forth in a Notice of Addition for a particular Phase. The Neighborhood Association shall maintain all Neighborhood Association Property. The Neighborhood Association shall repair and pay for all centrally metered utilities and mechanical and electrical equipment serving the Neighborhood Association Property. The Neighborhood Association shall perform periodic structural repair, resurfacing, sealing, caulking, replacement or painting of the Exclusive Use Area balconies, so long as the need for any of these activities is not caused by the willful or negligent acts of the Owner of the Unit to which the Exclusive Use Area is appurtenant or any of such Owner's Family, tenants or guests. The Neighborhood Association shall maintain, repair, replace, and periodically service, as necessary, all fire sprinkler systems and water lines which serve the residential building structures in the Neighborhood. The Neighborhood Association shall also paint, stain and waterproof, as necessary, the main entry doors to, and the garage doors of each Unit. The Neighborhood Association's maintenance responsibility includes the obligation to maintain all surfaces and the structural integrity of any and all walls and fences installed by Declarant in the Neighborhood which enclose the Exclusive Use Area balconies. The Neighborhood Association shall also be responsible for maintaining the clustered mailboxes for the Neighborhood.

#### (d) Reused/Recycled Green Waste

To the greatest extent practicable, and if commercially and reasonably feasible, the Neighborhood Association shall ensure, through its landscaping contracts or otherwise, that the green waste from Neighborhood Association landscape maintenance will be diverted from landfills in support of City Council Resolution No. 07-95 for Zero Waste, a copy of which shall be kept on file by the Neighborhood Association at all times.

#### (e) Pest Eradication

If determined by the Board to be economically feasible, the Neighborhood Association shall adopt an inspection and prevention program for the prevention and eradication of infestation by wood-destroying pests and organisms in the Neighborhood. The Neighborhood Association may, but is not required to, perform all corrective janitorial, landscaping and repair work in any Condominium if the Owner thereof fails to do so after Notice and Hearing. If the Neighborhood Association adopts an inspection and preventive program for the prevention and eradication of infestation by wood destroying pests and organisms, the Neighborhood Association, on no less than fifteen (15) nor more than thirty (30) days' notice, may require each Owner and any occupants of the Owner's Condominium to vacate such Condominium to accommodate Neighborhood Association efforts to eradicate such infestation. The notice must state the reason for the temporary relocation, the date and time of the beginning of treatment, the anticipated date and time of termination of treatment, and that the occupants will be responsible for their own accommodations during the temporary relocation. Any damage caused to a Condominium by such entry by the Neighborhood Association or by any person authorized by the Neighborhood Association shall be repaired by the Neighborhood Association as a Common Expense. All costs involved in operating the inspection and prevention program as well as repairing and replacing the Neighborhood Association Property and Improvements thereon when the need for such maintenance, repair or replacement is the result of wood destroying pests or organisms are a Common Expense.

#### (f) Additional Items

The Neighborhood Association shall also be responsible for maintaining any Improvements a majority of the voting power of the Neighborhood Association designates for maintenance by the Neighborhood Association. Such property shall be deemed to be Neighborhood Association Property and subject to the Governing Documents applicable to the Neighborhood Association Property.

#### (g) Charges to Owners

All costs of maintenance, repairs and replacements for the Neighborhood shall be paid for as Common Expenses out of the Neighborhood Association Maintenance Funds as provided in this Neighborhood Declaration.

### 2.1.4 Disputes Regarding Maintenance Obligations

If a dispute arises between Owners or between an Owner and the Neighborhood Association regarding any maintenance obligation, the Board, in its reasonable discretion, shall determine who is responsible for the maintenance obligation and that decision shall be binding on the Neighborhood Association and all Owners.

### 2.1.5 Inspection of the Neighborhood

#### (a) Inspection by Declarant

Except as otherwise provided in this Neighborhood Declaration, from and after the date of conveyance of fee title or appropriate easements thereto to the Neighborhood Association, the Neighborhood Association will be responsible for the maintenance, repair, replacement, irrigation, brush clearance, landscaping and preservation of the appearance of the Neighborhood Association Property in strict compliance with the Maintenance Manual provided to the Board of the Neighborhood Association by Declarant and in accordance with all commonly accepted maintenance practices. To ensure compliance with the requirements of the Maintenance Manual, for a period of ten (10) years after the Close of Escrow for the sale of the last Condominium in the Neighborhood covered by a Public Report, Declarant shall have the right, but not the obligation, at its own cost and expense, to retain the services of appropriate consultant(s) to (a) prepare, update and keep current, the Maintenance Manual for the Neighborhood Association maintenance, repair and replacement of the Neighborhood Association Property, (b) conduct annual inspections of all elements of the Neighborhood Association Property covered by the Maintenance Manual, and (c) prepare a report covering the results of such inspections and deliver such report to Declarant and to the Neighborhood Association. Declarant hereby reserves nonexclusive easements on, over, under, across and through all Neighborhood Association Property within the Neighborhood, for the purpose of such inspections and activities related thereto. The Neighborhood Association shall provide Declarant's consultant(s) with copies of its maintenance log, and related records reasonably requested by such consultant(s), at least thirty (30) days prior to the date scheduled for the Declarant's annual inspection. Declarant shall provide any updates to the Maintenance Manual to the Neighborhood Association. The Neighborhood Association shall cause such Neighborhood Association Property to be regularly maintained, painted, repaired, and/or replaced in accordance with the requirements and recommendations of the Maintenance Manual, as revised from time to time and shall perform all remedial maintenance in accordance with the recommendations of the annual inspection reports prepared by Declarant's consultant(s). The provisions of this Section shall not be amended without the prior written consent of Declarant.

#### (b) Maintenance Manual, Compliance

The Neighborhood Association has the duty and obligation, along with the attendant rights and power to carry out the Declarant's and its consultant(s)' required maintenance of the Neighborhood Association Property, as set forth in the Maintenance Manual. The Board shall regularly determine whether the recommended inspections and maintenance activities have been followed, and, if any such recommendations have not been followed, what corrective steps need to be taken to assure proper inspections and maintenance of the Neighborhood Association Property. The Board shall keep a record of such determinations in the Board's minutes. The Board shall review the Maintenance Manual for any needed appropriate revisions at appropriate intervals, but in no event less frequently than annually, and shall make appropriate recommendations to Declarant and Declarant's consultant(s) for revisions to the Maintenance Manual within thirty (30) days of its determination that revisions to the Maintenance Manual should be recommended to Declarant.

#### (c) Neighborhood Association Inspections

If in any year Declarant elects not to perform an annual maintenance inspection as provided for in Section 2.1.5(a) above, the Board shall have the duty and obligation to cause an inspection and report to be made in accordance with the provisions hereof. The Board's annual inspections shall (i) determine whether the Neighborhood Association Property is being maintained adequately in accordance with the standards of maintenance established herein and by the Maintenance Manual, (ii) identify the condition of the Neighborhood Association Property, including the existence of any hazards or defects, and the need for performing additional maintenance, repair, refurbishing or replacement, and (iii) recommend preventative actions, which may be taken to reduce potential maintenance costs to be incurred in the future. The Neighborhood Association may employ such experts and consultants as necessary to perform such inspections. Within thirty (30) days after the Board's annual inspection, the Board shall have a report of the results of the inspection prepared, and such report shall include the following: (i) a description of the condition of the Neighborhood Association Property, including a list of items inspected and the status of maintenance, repair and need for replacement of all such items; (ii) a description of all maintenance, repair and replacement planned for the ensuing fiscal year, and if any maintenance, repair or replacement is to be deferred, an explanation must be given for such deferral; (iii) a summary of all reports of inspections performed by any expert or consultant employed by the Neighborhood Association to perform inspections; (iv) a report of the status of compliance with the maintenance, replacement and repair needs set forth in all inspection reports from Declarant's consultant(s) and of its own inspection, for preceding years; and (v) such other matters as the Board deems appropriate. The Board shall promptly cause a copy of each inspection report prepared in accordance with this Section to be delivered to Declarant. The Neighborhood Association's obligations under this Section shall continue until the expiration of the ten (10) year period following the Close of Escrow for the sale of the last Condominium in the Neighborhood covered by a Public Report. The requirements of this Section are in addition to the Board's obligations to perform ongoing reserve studies as required by the Bylaws. The provisions of this Section shall not be amended without the prior written consent of Declarant.

### 2.1.6 Damage by Owners

Each Owner is liable to the Neighborhood Association for all damage to the Neighborhood Association Property that is sustained due to the negligence or willful act of the Owner, the Owner's Family, tenants or invitees, and any other Persons who derive their use of the Neighborhood Association Property from the Owner or from the Owner's Family, tenants or invitees. The Neighborhood Association may, after Notice and Hearing, levy a Special Assessment against the Owner representing a monetary charge imposed as a means of reimbursing the Neighborhood Association for costs incurred by the Neighborhood Association in the repair of damage to Neighborhood Association Property and facilities for which the Owner or the Owner's Family, tenants or invitees were responsible. The amount of the Special Assessment may include (a) the amount of any deductible payable on the insured portion of the loss (if the Neighborhood Association elects to make a claim under its insurance policy), (b) all costs and expenses actually incurred by the Neighborhood Association to correct damage that is not covered by the Neighborhood Association's insurance or for which no claim has been made, and (c) the amount of the increase in premiums payable by the Neighborhood Association, to the extent the increase is directly caused by damage that was attributed to the Owner or the Owner's Family, tenants or invitees. In accordance with the CID Act, the Neighborhood Association shall have the power to impose a lien for the foregoing Special Assessment. If a Condominium is jointly owned, the liability of its Owners for damage to Neighborhood Association Property is joint and several, except to the extent that the Neighborhood Association and the joint Owners have otherwise agreed in writing.

### 2.1.7 Roof Access

Air conditioner compressor pads for certain Condominiums in the Neighborhood are located on the rooftops of the buildings containing such Condominiums. Owners shall not access the rooftops of buildings by any method. Access to rooftops is strictly limited to qualified air conditioning personnel and Neighborhood Association agents and employees. The Neighborhood Association may establish a list of pre-approved repair technicians in the Neighborhood Guidelines.

## 2.2 Exclusive Use Area Stairwells

No Owner may paint, alter, or modify in any fashion any Exclusive Use Area stairwell in the Neighborhood without the prior written approval of the Architectural Review Committee. Each Exclusive Use Area stairwell is designed to provide emergency egress from the appurtenant Unit; hence there shall be no storage of personal property within any Exclusive Use Area stairwell or any alteration to the stairwell walls or ceiling, landings, rails, stairs, doors, and/or other Improvements within the Exclusive Use Area stairwell that might impede access through the stairwell. Each Owner shall be responsible to maintain, repair and replace any handrail within the stairwell, as well as all door hardware and any doors providing access to and egress from the stairwell (though replacement of any door leading to the exterior of the building shall require the prior written approval of the Architectural Review Committee).

## 2.3 Exclusive Use Area Balconies

Wherever a balcony is attached to a Unit, the Owner shall not have the right, without the approval of the Board or its designee, to paint, alter, remodel or enclose, any such balcony and then only in a manner which does not impair the uniform appearance of such balcony in comparison with other balconies within the Neighborhood. Each Owner shall be responsible to maintain, service, repair and replace all glass doors and windows enclosing such Owner's balcony, if any, together with such related parts and equipment as are reasonably necessary. Each Owner of a Unit which has a balcony attached to it shall have the right to furnish such balcony with attractive outdoor furniture in keeping with the architecture of the Neighborhood and reasonable family use, and shall keep such balcony in a clean and sanitary condition. Awnings, sunshades, umbrellas and the like are not permitted. In no event shall unsightly objects be placed or stored on a balcony where they may be seen by other Owners from their Units, balconies or using the Neighborhood Association Property, or by the public in general.

## 2.4 Noise from Balconies

Each Owner and any other occupant of a Unit must be sensitive to and considerate of the rights of other Owners and occupants to the quiet use and enjoyment of their respective Units. The use of any balcony in the Neighborhood must be restricted to reasonable and quiet activities that do not disturb neighbors, and reasonable hours of use. The Board may impose reasonable restrictions on the use of balconies to preserve the peaceful enjoyment by all Owners of their Units, and may adopt a system of monetary penalties and fines to enforce such rules.

## 2.5 Plants

No Owner shall place, hang or cause to grow any live plant on top of any wall or fence in the Neighborhood, including any wall or fence enclosing or partially enclosing an Exclusive Use Area or any outer building wall enclosing the Unit itself. No Owner may penetrate any such wall or fence to hang a plant. Live plants in pots may be kept in Exclusive Use Area balconies provided that they are kept in pots equipped with a saucer or similar water catching device underneath it to prevent water from pooling on the Exclusive Use Area floor. In order to protect the stucco on building exteriors, Owners must avoid allowing water to run down building walls. Live plants are not allowed on small balconies with no access, on pot shelves or on wall caps. All other items requiring water may not be kept in any Exclusive Use Area balconies in the Neighborhood.

## 2.6 Water Beds and Aquariums

No waterbeds shall be permitted in any Condominium. No Owner may maintain in his or her Condominium any aquarium or other similar container holding thirty (30) or more gallons of water. Each Owner acknowledges that substantial damage to other Units and/or Neighborhood Association Property may occur as a result of a violation of this restriction.

## 2.7 Single Family Residence

Each Residence shall be used as a dwelling for a single Family and for no other purpose. The Neighborhood Association Property, including parking spaces and other amenities contemplated as a part of the Neighborhood, will not be leased by Declarant to the Owners or the Neighborhood Association. Owners may also rent Condominiums to Declarant for use as sales offices, models and parking areas. Any failure by a tenant of a Condominium to comply with the Governing Documents constitutes a default under the lease or rental agreement.

## 2.8 Lease and Rent Restrictions

Any Owner may lease or rent the Owner's Unit. No Owner shall be permitted to lease or rent such Owner's Unit for transient purposes, hotel purposes or for any purposes inconsistent with this Neighborhood Declaration. Any lease or rental agreement that is either for a period of fewer than sixty (60) days or pursuant to which the Owner or tenant (under a sublease) provides any services normally associated with a hotel, shall be deemed to be for transient or hotel purposes and violate this Section 2.7. Any lease or rental agreement is required to provide that the terms of said lease or rental shall be subject in all respects to the provisions of this Neighborhood Declaration, the Master Declaration and any Rules and Regulations adopted by the Neighborhood Association and Community Association and that any failure by the lessee or tenant to comply with the terms of such documents shall be a default under the lease or rental agreement. The Owner shall, at all times, be responsible for their tenant's or lessee's compliance with all of the provisions of this Neighborhood Declaration and the Master Declaration pursuant to the occupancy and use of the Condominium. A lessee or tenant shall have no obligation to the Community Association or the Neighborhood Association to pay assessments imposed by the Community Association or the Neighborhood Association, nor shall any lessee or tenant have any voting rights in the Community Association or the Neighborhood Association. A copy of this Neighborhood Declaration, the Master Declaration and any Rules and Regulations affecting the Neighborhood shall be made available to each tenant or lessee by the Owner of the leased or rented Unit. All leases and rental agreement are required to be in writing.

## 2.9 Further Subdivision

Except as otherwise provided in this Neighborhood Declaration, no Owner may physically or legally subdivide his Condominium in any manner, including dividing such Owner's Condominium into time-share estates or time-share uses. This provision does not limit the right of an Owner to (a) rent or lease his entire Condominium by a written lease or rental agreement subject to this Neighborhood Declaration, (b) sell such Owner's Condominium, or (c) transfer or sell any Condominium to more than one (1) Person to be held by them as tenants-in-common, joint tenants, tenants by the entirety or as Neighborhood Association Property.

## 2.10 Business and Commercial Activities

Subject to Section 2.1 of the Master Declaration, no part of the Neighborhood may ever be used for any business, commercial (including auctions or similar events), manufacturing, mercantile, storage, soliciting, vending or other nonresidential purposes, including any activity for which the provider is compensated or receives any consideration, regardless of whether the activity is engaged in full or part-time, generates or does not generate a profit, or requires or does not require a license. This Section does not preclude any of the above-described activities provided that: (a) the activity complies with law; (b) the patrons or clientele of the activity do not visit the Condominium or park automobiles or other vehicles in the Neighborhood; (c) the existence or operation of the activity is not apparent or detectable by sight, sound or smell from outside the boundaries of the Condominium; (d) the activity does not increase the Neighborhood Association's liability or casualty insurance obligation or premium; and (e) the activity is consistent with the residential character of the Neighborhood and this Neighborhood Declaration.

## 2.11 Nuisances

Noxious and offensive activities in the Neighborhood or on any public street abutting or visible from the Neighborhood are prohibited. All horns, whistles, bells or other sound devices, except security devices used exclusively to protect the security of a Condominium or vehicle and its contents, are also prohibited. Noisy, unsightly, unusually painted or smoky vehicles, large power equipment and large power tools (excluding lawn mowers and other equipment used in connection with ordinary landscape maintenance), off-road motor vehicles and objects which create or emit loud noises or noxious odors may not be located, used or placed in the Neighborhood or on any public street abutting or visible from the Neighborhood, or exposed to the view of other Owners without the Board's written approval. Any item which would unreasonably interfere with television or radio reception to a Condominium may be located or used in the Neighborhood or on a public street abutting the Neighborhood only with the prior written consent of the Board. The Neighborhood Association is entitled to determine if any noise, odor, or activity producing such noise or odor constitutes a nuisance. The Neighborhood Association may unilaterally delegate responsibility for enforcing this Section, in whole or in part, to the Owners. No Owner may (a) permit or cause anything to be done or kept in the Neighborhood or on any public street abutting or visible from the Neighborhood which may (i) increase the rate of insurance in the Neighborhood, (ii) result in the cancellation of such insurance, or (iii) obstruct or interfere with the rights of other Owners, or (b) commit or permit any nuisance thereon or violate any law. Each Owner shall comply with all requirements of the local or state health authorities and with all other laws regarding occupancy and use of a Condominium. Each Owner is accountable to the Neighborhood Association and other Owners for the conduct of persons residing in or visiting the Owner's Condominium. Any damage to the Neighborhood Association Property, personal property of the Neighborhood Association, or property of another Owner caused by such persons shall be repaired at the sole expense of the Owner of the Condominium where such persons are residing or visiting. No plants or seeds infected with noxious insects or plant diseases may be brought upon, grown or maintained in the Neighborhood.

## 2.12 Signs

Subject to Civil Code Sections 712, 713 and 4710, no sign, advertising device or other display of any kind shall be displayed in the Neighborhood Association Property or on any public street in or abutting the Neighborhood except for: (i) entry monuments, community identification signs, and traffic or parking control signs maintained by the Neighborhood Association, (ii) one (1) nameplate or similar Owner name or address identification sign for each Condominium which complies with the Design Guidelines, and (iii) noncommercial signs, posters, flags or banners on or in the Condominium, except as required for the protection of public health or safety or if posting or display would violate a local, State or Federal law. A noncommercial sign, poster, flag or banner may be made of paper, cardboard, cloth, plastic or fabric, and may be posted or displayed only from a window, door, balcony or other areas within exclusive control of the Owner. Such noncommercial signs, posters, flags and banners may not be made of lights, roofing, siding, paving materials, flora, or balloons or any other similar building, landscaping or decorative component, or include the painting of architectural surfaces. The Neighborhood Association may prohibit noncommercial signs and posters that are more than nine (9) square feet in size and noncommercial flags or banners that are more than fifteen (15) square feet in size. No sign advertising the Condominium for sale or lease may be placed on the Neighborhood Association Property. The preferred location for such sign shall be in a window.

## 2.13 Holiday Decorations

Outdoor holiday decorations, or indoor holiday decorations that are visible from outside, shall be limited to a reasonable period of time prior to the date of the holiday, as determined by the Community Association and the Neighborhood Association in their respective Rules and Regulations and Neighborhood Guidelines, and shall be removed within no more than fourteen (14) days after such holiday, unless prior written authorization has been granted by the Community Association or the Neighborhood Association, as applicable. Owners shall not pierce the exterior surface of the building for the purpose of displaying holiday decorations, or for any other reason.

## 2.14 Parking and Vehicular Restrictions

### 2.14.1 Authorized Vehicles

The following vehicles are "Authorized Vehicles:" (a) standard passenger vehicles, including automobiles, vehicles designed to accommodate ten (10) or fewer people, (b) motorcycles and (c) pick-up trucks having a payload capacity of one (1) ton or less. Authorized Vehicles may be parked in any portion of the Neighborhood intended for parking of motorized vehicles; however, no Owner may park a vehicle in a manner which the Neighborhood Association determines either restricts the passage of pedestrians or vehicles over garage areas, streets or sidewalks in the Neighborhood. The Neighborhood Association has the power to identify additional vehicles as Authorized Vehicles in the Neighborhood Guidelines to adapt this restriction to other types of vehicles.

### 2.14.2 Prohibited Vehicles

The following vehicles are "Prohibited Vehicles": (a) recreational vehicles (including motorhomes, travel trailers, camper vans, jet skis, motor boats and other motorized vehicles designed for travel over water), (b) commercial-type vehicles (including pick-up trucks having a payload capacity in excess of one (1) ton, stakebed trucks, tank trucks, dump trucks, step vans, concrete trucks, limousines, vehicles with commercial signage and other commercial purpose vehicles) or vehicles with advertisements, placards or other writing on the vehicle or any equipment on a vehicle used for any commercial purpose, (c) vehicles designed to accommodate more than ten (10) people, (d) vehicles having more than two (2) axles, (e) trailers, inoperable vehicles or parts of vehicles, (f) other similar vehicles, or (g) any vehicle or vehicular equipment deemed a nuisance by the Board.

Prohibited Vehicles may not be parked, stored or kept on any public or private street in, adjacent to or visible from the Neighborhood unless (i) they are owned and used by the Community Association or the Neighborhood Association in connection with management or maintenance of a part of the Neighborhood, (ii) they are parked for brief periods as may be defined in the Neighborhood Guidelines (such as loading, unloading, making deliveries or emergency repairs), or (iii) they are parked in an Owner's fully enclosed garage with the door closed.

Prohibited Vehicles may be parked in an Owner's garage only if, and to the extent that, an Owner has fewer Authorized Vehicles than the number of vehicles the garage was originally designed to hold. No Owner may fill a garage with a Prohibited Vehicle or Vehicles and park an Authorized Vehicle or Vehicles on a street. If a vehicle qualifies as both an Authorized Vehicle and a Prohibited Vehicle, then the vehicle is presumed to be a Prohibited Vehicle, unless the vehicle is expressly classified as an Authorized Vehicle in writing by the Board. The Neighborhood Association has the power, but not the duty, to identify additional vehicles as Prohibited Vehicles in the Neighborhood Guidelines to adapt this restriction to new types of vehicles produced by manufacturers.

### 2.14.3 Garage and Parking Restrictions

Residential garages shall be solely used for parking purposes, unless as allowed in Section 2.13.3(a) below. The garage shall be used to park the number of Authorized Vehicles the garage was designed to accommodate and shall not be used for storage, living purposes (for people or animals) or recreational activities of any kind. Except for temporary loading and unloading, no Owner shall leave his or her Authorized Vehicle parked or left within the Neighborhood other than within such Owner's garage, provided that such Authorized Vehicle does not encroach into the sidewalk area.

#### (a) Garage Restrictions

(i) Owners may not use any space within the garage for temporary or permanent living purposes, regardless of the number of vehicles the Owner possesses;

(ii) Owners may not, under any circumstances, use the garage as a temporary or permanent living space for animals of any kind, including but not limited to, dogs, cats, rodents (e.g., rats, mice and hamsters), rabbits and reptiles (e.g., snakes and lizards);

(iii) Garage doors shall be kept closed;

(iv) Each Owner shall be responsible for ensuring that their family members comply with the restrictions and requirements set forth in this Neighborhood Declaration, any Supplemental Neighborhood Declaration and any additional Neighborhood Guidelines;

(v) Owner and Owner's family members may be prohibited from parking on any Neighborhood Association Property street in the Neighborhood;

(vi) Any Owner whose vehicle (including vehicles belonging to any members of the Owner's family) is found to be in violation of this Section 2.13 shall be subject to towing, fines as permitted by law, and any other disciplinary action the Board may promulgate, including the subsequent verification of adherence to this Section 2.13 which may include the right to visually inspect the interior of the violating Owner's garage; and

(vii) The Neighborhood Association shall have the right, but not the obligation, to establish procedures in the Neighborhood Guidelines to enforce and verify adherence to the parking and garage restrictions and requirements in this Section, which include the right of the Neighborhood Association and Community Association to conduct inspections of the garages.

#### (b) Private Street Parking

Pursuant to the Cost Sharing Agreement, guest parking spaces within the Private Streets are intended to be used solely by guests; however, subject to the Cost Sharing Agreement the Board may promulgate reasonable and nondiscriminatory rules and regulations allowing Owners and other residents of the Neighborhood and Rockefeller to use guest parking spaces within the Private Streets. Guests of Owners and other residents must park their vehicles either in the Owner's garage or, subject to the Cost Sharing Agreement and any rule promulgated by the Neighborhood Association, designated parking areas on the Private Streets. When present in the Private Streets, vehicles must not interfere with normal use of the Private Streets by other Owners, residents or visitors. Private Street parking spaces are for temporary use only. Parking on the Private Streets is subject to the Cost Sharing Agreement, applicable law, including the City's Municipal Code and the California Vehicle Code. Vehicles must be moved to accommodate street sweeping. Violating vehicles may be ticketed or towed. Pursuant to the Cost Sharing Agreement, parking on the Private Streets is unreserved and unassigned, and it is available on a first-come-first-served basis.

### 2.14.4 Repair, Maintenance and Restoration

No Person may repair, maintain or restore any vehicle in the Neighborhood, unless the work is conducted in the garage with the garage door closed. Such an activity may be prohibited entirely by the Board if the Board determines that it constitutes a nuisance. However, no Person may carry on in any portion of the Neighborhood any vehicle repair or maintenance (except in an emergency) or restoration business.

### 2.14.5 Electric Vehicles and Charging Stations

Declarant and the Neighborhood Association may designate specific areas in the Neighborhood for the parking or recharging of electric vehicles. The Neighborhood Association may define "electric vehicles" in the Neighborhood Guidelines and establish additional parking and use restrictions for such vehicles. The Neighborhood Association may adopt regulations establishing parking and use restrictions for electric vehicles that may be more restrictive or less restrictive than regulations on other Authorized Vehicles. In addition, the Neighborhood Guidelines may establish regulations, requirements and/or restrictions on installation of electric vehicle charging stations on any portion of the Neighborhood Association Property, so long as the restrictions comply with federal, state and local law.

### 2.14.6 Enforcement

The Board has the power, but not the duty, to enforce all parking and vehicle use regulations applicable to the Neighborhood, including the removal of violating vehicles from Private Streets and other portions of the Neighborhood in accordance with California Vehicle Code Section 22658 or other applicable laws. The City may, but is not required to, enforce such restrictions, rules and regulations, in addition to applicable laws and ordinances. The Board shall take all necessary actions to enforce the provisions of this Section 2.13 upon request by the City Director of Community Development.

### 2.14.7 Regulation and Restriction by Board

Subject to the Cost Sharing Agreement, the Board has the power to: (a) establish additional rules and regulations concerning parking in the Neighborhood Association Property, including designating "parking," "guest parking," and "no parking" areas; (b) prohibit any vehicle parking, operation, repair, maintenance or restoration activity in the Neighborhood if it determines in its sole discretion that the activity is a nuisance; and (c) promulgate rules and regulations concerning vehicles and parking in the Neighborhood as it deems necessary and desirable.

## 2.15 Animal Regulations

The only animals that may be raised, bred or kept in the Condominium are dogs, cats, fish, birds and other usual household pets; provided that they are not kept or bred for commercial purposes, in unreasonable quantities, or in violation of the Governing Documents. As used in this Neighborhood Declaration, "unreasonable quantities" means more than two (2) pets per household; provided, however, that the Board may determine that a reasonable number in any instance may be more or less. The Board may limit the size of pets and may prohibit maintenance of any animal which, in the Board's opinion, constitutes a nuisance. An Owner may also keep, subject to prior approval of the Board, an "exotic animal." Notwithstanding the foregoing, if any Owner's or other resident's animal has exhibited aggressive behavior (e.g., has attacked or attempted to attack any person or a common domesticated household animal in the Neighborhood) that animal is subject to removal from the Neighborhood in accordance with City animal control regulations and/or the Neighborhood Association's Rules and Regulations. Any Owner desiring to keep an "exotic animal" shall make prior application to the Board for permission to keep an "exotic animal." As used herein, the term "exotic animal" shall mean any type of snake or reptile which can grow to a length longer than two (2) feet, any type of spider, any animal which is poisonous or which would pose a risk of harm to any person or to a common domesticated household animal if such exotic animal escaped from its respective Unit, and any other animal which may be designated from time to time as an "exotic animal" by the Board. The Board shall give reasonable notice and hold a hearing on an application to keep an exotic animal and shall give at least five (5) days prior written notice of such hearing to the applicant, to the applicant's adjoining Owners and to such other Owners within the Neighborhood as the Board may deem appropriate. The Board may in its sole and absolute discretion approve or disapprove such application, and may also impose such conditions upon the right to keep an exotic animal as it may deem appropriate, including, without limitation, requiring the Owner to construct a secure enclosure to prevent the animal from escaping, to give written notice to other Owners of the presence of such exotic animal, to obtain additional liability insurance, and to reimburse the Association for any costs incurred as the result of the animal escaping. In all cases, all animals may only be kept in accordance with applicable City ordinances and codes, including without limitation City of Irvine Municipal Code Section 4-5-1007 "Animals at Large", and may not be kept, bred or maintained for any commercial purpose or in unreasonable numbers as determined by the Board, from time to time, which may be more restrictive than allowed by City ordinances. Each Owner shall be responsible for cleaning up any excrement or other unclean or unsanitary condition caused by the Owner's animal(s) anywhere within the Neighborhood. All animals must be kept either within the Unit or on a leash held by a person capable of controlling the animal. No animals may be kept in any Exclusive Use Area balcony. Any Owner claiming that an animal is dangerous or constitutes a nuisance shall first direct such complaint to the City and/or County animal control department. Upon the approval of a majority of a quorum of the Board, the Board may prohibit the maintenance of any animal (including any common domesticated household animal or any previously approved exotic animal) which, after Notice and Hearing, is determined by the Board to be a dangerous animal or to constitute a nuisance to any other person residing in the Neighborhood. Every person keeping an animal within, or bringing an animal into, the Neighborhood shall be liable pursuant to the laws of the State of California to any and all persons for any injury to persons or damage to property caused by such animal. Any Person who keeps any animal, insect or reptile in the Neighborhood, whether in compliance with or in violation of the Governing Documents, shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless the Neighborhood Association, its officers, directors, contractors, agents and employees from any claim brought by any Person against the Neighborhood Association, its officers, directors, agents and employees for personal injuries or property damage caused by such animal, insect or reptile.

## 2.16 Antenna Restrictions

### 2.16.1 Authorized Antenna

No Person may install on the exterior of any Residence, rooftops, or balcony railings any antenna or over-the-air receiving device except for an "Authorized Antenna." An Authorized Antenna is (a) an antenna designed to receive direct broadcast satellite service, including direct-to-home satellite service, that is one (1) meter or less in diameter, (b) an antenna designed to receive video programming service, including multichannel multipoint distribution service, instructional television fixed service, and local multipoint distribution service, and is one (1) meter or less in diameter or diagonal measurement, (c) an antenna designed to receive television broadcast signals, or (d) an antenna used to receive and transmit fixed wireless signals. An Authorized Antenna may only be mounted on a tripod, which may not be fastened to a building surface such as to cause damage thereto, to reach the height needed to receive an acceptable quality signal, subject to local governmental agency permitting requirements for safety purposes.

### 2.16.2 Restrictions on Installation

The Board may adopt reasonable restrictions on installation and use of an Authorized Antenna as part of its Design Guidelines in order to minimize visibility of the Authorized Antenna from other Condominiums. Such restrictions may designate one (1) or more preferred installation locations, or require camouflage such as paint (subject to the antenna manufacturer's recommendations) or screening vegetation or other Improvements. However, no restriction imposed by the Committee may (a) unreasonably delay or prevent the installation, maintenance or use of an Authorized Antenna, (b) unreasonably increase the cost of the installation, maintenance or use of an Authorized Antenna, or (c) preclude acceptable quality reception.

### 2.16.3 Prohibitions on Installation

No Owner shall install any antenna or other Improvement on the building exterior or on any real property which such Owner does not own, such as the walls and fences, if applicable, enclosing the Owner's Exclusive Use Areas, or is not entitled to exclusively use or control under the Governing Documents without the prior written approval of the Board, which it may withhold at its sole discretion. The Board may prohibit the installation of an Authorized Antenna in a particular location if in the Board's opinion, the installation, location or maintenance of such Authorized Antenna unreasonably affects the safety of the Owners or any other Person, or for any other safety-related reason established by the Board. The Architectural Review Committee also has the power to prohibit or restrict the installation of any antenna or other over-the-air receiving device that does not meet the definition of an Authorized Antenna above.

### 2.16.4 Review after Installation

Subject to the Board's prior review of any installation of an Authorized Antenna which affects the building exterior as described in Section 2.15.3 above, the Board may review the location and installation of an Authorized Antenna after it is installed on a tripod in the Owner's Exclusive Use Area. After its review, the Board may require that the Authorized Antenna be moved to a preferred location (if one has been designated) for safety reasons or to comply with reasonable restrictions subject to this Section and applicable law.

### 2.16.5 Restatement of Applicable Law

This Section is intended to be a restatement of the authority granted to the Neighborhood Association and Committee under the law. All amendments, modifications, restatements and interpretations of the law applicable to the installation, use or maintenance of an antenna or over-the-air receiving device shall be interpreted to amend, modify, restate or interpret this Section.

## 2.17 Trash

Trash must be stored within the Neighborhood in accordance with all provisions of the Master Declaration. Storage of building materials, refuse or any other materials in the Neighborhood is prohibited, except building materials may be kept in areas designated by the Board temporarily during construction. The trash containers for a Unit shall be stored in the Unit's garage, and may only be set out at the curbside directly behind the Unit or the Unit's garage door for a reasonable period of time for trash collection purposes (not to exceed twelve (12) hours before and after scheduled trash collection hours). The trash containers for a Unit may not be set out at the curbside behind any other Unit. By accepting a deed to a Unit, each Owner agrees that the Owner and the other occupants of a Unit may be required to move the Unit's trash containers as necessary on trash collection days for purposes of ingress to and egress from the Unit's garage, and further covenant that the Unit's trash containers shall not be placed behind another Unit (including another Unit's garage) except for the limited purpose of facilitating ingress to or egress from the Unit's garage, and then only for such limited time as necessary to facilitate such ingress or egress. Failure to strictly abide by this Section 2.16 may result in disciplinary action against a Unit's Owner by the Board. Furthermore, in no event shall such trash containers be stored in any Exclusive Use Area of any Condominium. Trash containers owned by the Neighborhood Association may be kept on Neighborhood Association Property so long as they are contained in an enclosure installed by Declarant or approved by the Design Review Committee (as defined in the Master Declaration).

## 2.18 Installations

Except for subsection 2.17.4, this Section 2.17 does not apply to Improvements installed (a) as a part of the original construction of the Neighborhood by Declarant, (b) by the Neighborhood Association, or (c) with the approval of the Architectural Review Committee.

### 2.18.1 Outside Installations

The following outside installations are prohibited: (a) sound systems, clotheslines, balcony covers, wiring, air conditioning equipment, water softeners, other machines and other similar Improvements, (b) Improvements protruding through the walls or roofs of buildings, and (c) other exterior additions or alterations to any Condominium. Outdoor patio or lounge furniture may be kept in the Exclusive Use Areas in accordance with the Design Guidelines and any approval requirements of the Architectural Review Committee. No materials may be stored in the Exclusive Use Areas. Barbecues are not allowed on the balconies. No clothing, fabrics or unsightly articles may be hung, dried or aired on or over any Condominium. Articles which are unsightly may be defined in the Neighborhood Guidelines. No exterior roof mounted mechanical equipment, poles or masts shall be constructed on or attached to any residential dwelling or erected or maintained on any Unit or balcony. No temporary or permanent basketball standard or backboard, or other sports apparatus shall be constructed, erected, installed or maintained on any Unit or balcony, or on any Neighborhood Association Property, including Private Streets, in the Neighborhood.

### 2.18.2 Inside Installations

Subject to extension by the Board, Owners may cover their windows with white sheets for up to three (3) months prior to installing permanent window coverings. Permanent window coverings must have either a white or cream color backing or may be white or light-colored natural wood shutters. All window coverings shall be of a uniform neutral color harmonious with and not in conflict with the color scheme of the exterior wall surface of the building. No Owner shall be permitted to tint any windows in the Unit which are visible from the exterior of the building. Window coverings shall be subject to the approval of the Architectural Review Committee. Nothing may be done in any Condominium or in, on or to the Neighborhood Association Property which may impair the structural integrity of any building in the Neighborhood or which structurally alters any such building except as otherwise expressly provided in this Neighborhood Declaration. No alteration, repair or replacement of wall coverings in Condominiums which may diminish the effectiveness of the sound control engineering in the buildings in the Neighborhood may be made.

### 2.18.3 Noise Mitigation

No Owner may take any actions that may interfere with structural noise mitigation Improvements installed in the Condominium by Declarant. Owners are further prohibited from (a) puncturing, piercing or otherwise altering any walls or ceilings shared with another Condominium, if any, and (b) installing any lighting, sound system, loudspeakers, entertainment system or other music-, sound- or noise-generating or amplifying device in any walls or ceiling of an attached Condominium. Owners shall not install hard surface flooring in any upper level bedroom areas as shown on Exhibit D. Owners shall also not install hard surface flooring in any upper level bedroom without the prior written approval of the Architectural Review Committee. Owners may replace hard surface flooring installed by Declarant, provided the Owner replaces the flooring with materials that are the same as or substantially similar to the original materials installed by the Declarant (including underlayment as applicable). If an Owner installs hard surface flooring in any portion of the Residence without installing materials which are the same as or substantially similar to the original materials installed by the Declarant, the Neighborhood Association may require the Owner of such Unit to remove the flooring at the Owner's sole cost and expense and re-install the flooring with materials which are the same as or substantially similar to the original materials installed by the Declarant.

### 2.18.4 Sound Transmission Control

Each Owner must ensure that any changes to the Unit (especially plumbing and flooring) shall not have the effect of increasing the level of noise or sounds that can be heard outside of the Unit above a sound level (impact and airborne sounds) of 55 dB(A).

#### (a) Floors - In General

All changes to floors separating Units (tile, hardwood, stone, carpet, etc.) must provide code-compliant sound control properties for airborne and impact sound insulation. In addition, the floor/ceiling assemblies must satisfy the higher sound control requirements established for the Neighborhood as set forth herein. The impact sound insulation rating of the floor ceiling assemblies after installation must be Field Impact Insulation Class ("FIIC") 50 or higher. Airborne sound isolation rating thereof must be Noise Isolation Class ("NIC") 52 or higher.

#### (b) Other Hard Surface Flooring Requirements

After installation of the original flooring in connection the first Owner's acquisition of a Unit from Declarant, no Owner shall install any hard surface flooring (including without limitation tile, stone or hardwood floors) or replace any flooring with any hard surface flooring, unless the prior approval of the Architectural Review Committee has been obtained. As a condition to approving the installation or replacement of hard surface flooring, the Owner shall submit to the Committee a construction drawing and specifications clearly indicating the type of flooring to be installed and the underlayment material to be provided to mitigate against impact noises such as footfalls and transmission of music and other sounds. The submission must clearly identify all materials, their composition and thickness, and include a report by an accredited acoustical testing laboratory showing that a test specimen essentially identical to the proposed assembly achieved a Sound Transmission Class ("STC") rating of not less than STC-54 and an Impact Insulation Class ("IIC") rating of not less than IIC-52. Following approval and installation of the new flooring assembly, a field test conducted under the supervision of a person experienced in the field of acoustical testing shall be performed in each room in which the flooring is installed to determine compliance with the requirements of NIC 52 or higher and FIIC 50 or higher. The required field test shall be completed within thirty (30) days after substantial completion of the work, subject to any extensions granted by the Committee or the Board. Non-compliant systems must be promptly brought into conformance or removed and replaced with approved materials/assemblies.

#### (c) Walls

Walls must provide an airborne sound insulation sufficient to meet an NIC rating of 52 and are not permitted to be altered.

#### (d) Plumbing

All plumbing must be properly insulated for sound and isolated from studs, joints, and penetrations.

#### (e) Penetrations

Penetrations or openings for piping, electrical devices, recessed cabinets, bathtubs, soffits or heating, ventilating or exhaust ducts shall be sealed, lined, insulated, or otherwise treated to maintain the required sound insulation ratings.

#### (f) Required Testing

All alterations to walls and floors must be field tested under the supervision of a person experienced in the field of acoustical testing to determine compliance with the minimum sound control standards set forth herein. Non-compliant systems must be promptly brought into conformance or removed and replaced with approved materials/assemblies.

### 2.18.5 Fire Sprinklers

The fire sprinkler system within a Unit is an integral part of the fire safety system serving other Units within the residential building. Any modification or attempt to modify such system within a Unit could affect the operation of the fire safety system within other Units and possibly the entire building. The sprinkler system is heat-sensitive and sprinkler heads should not be exposed to open flame or any other intense heat source. Fire sprinkler systems shall be maintained in accordance with Exhibit C and the provisions of any applicable Notice of Addition. Owners shall not paint over the fire sprinkler heads, attach anything to the fire sprinkler heads (for example, decorations) or hang anything from the fire sprinkler heads or any other exposed portion of the fire sprinkler system. By acceptance of a deed to a Condominium, each Owner acknowledges that Declarant and the Neighborhood Association are not responsible for (i) any damage to the Owners' Condominium or to the Neighborhood Association Property to the extent caused by an Owner's interference with or damage to fire sprinkler equipment, and (ii) any damage to the Owner's Condominium caused by the activation of the sprinkler system. The Neighborhood Association has the power to contract, provide and pay for any monitoring and maintenance of the fire sprinkler system that may be required, for the common use and benefit of all Owners. In such event, such Owner shall pay a proportionate share of the costs thereof as a Common Expense.

### 2.18.6 No Liability

Neither the Declarant nor the Neighborhood Association shall be liable or responsible for any damage that results from Improvements installed, constructed or modified by or at the direction of an Owner. Owners are advised to consult and use qualified consultants and contractors when installing, constructing or modifying Improvements on the Owner's Condominium.

No Owner may cause or permit any mechanic's lien to be filed against the Neighborhood Association Property or another Owner's Condominium for labor or materials alleged to have been furnished or delivered to such Owner. ## 2.25 Post-Tension Concrete System

Each Residence may be built using a post-tension concrete system ("System"). A placard or stamp will be placed in the garage signifying the existence of such System. The System involves placing steel cables under high tension in the concrete slab located beneath the Residence. Therefore, any attempt to alter or pierce the foundation (for example, sawing, cutting or drilling) could damage the integrity of the System and cause serious injury or damage to persons and property. Declarant shall not be responsible for any damage or injury resulting from or arising in connection with the alteration of the slab or foundation of any Residence. By acceptance of a deed to a Residence, Owners agree that: (i) Owners shall not cut into or otherwise tamper with the System; (ii) Owners shall not knowingly permit or allow any other person to cut into or tamper with the System so long as the Owner owns any interest in the Residence; (iii) Owners shall disclose the existence of the System to any person who rents, leases or purchases the Residence; and (iv) Owners shall indemnify and hold the Declarant and all of its affiliated entities, officers, directors, agents and assigns free and harmless from any and all claims, damages, losses or other liability (including attorneys' fees and costs) arising from any breach of this covenant.

## 2.26 Vapor Barrier System

Declarant has installed a vapor membrane (the "LIQUID BOOT®") to the foundations of the buildings within the Neighborhood as well as passive venting inside the building perimeter walls and out building roof vents. The LIQUID BOOT® is a water-based cold spray membrane used to prevent particles, moisture and vapor penetration into the Unit. By acceptance of a deed to a Residence, Owners agree that: (i) Owners shall not cut into or otherwise tamper with the LIQUID BOOT®; (ii) Owners shall not knowingly permit or allow any other person to cut into or tamper with the LIQUID BOOT® so long as the Owner owns any interest in the Residence; (iii) Owners shall disclose the existence of the LIQUID BOOT® to any person who rents, leases or purchases the Residence; and (iv) Owners shall indemnify and hold the Declarant and all of its affiliated entities, officers, directors, agents and assigns free and harmless from any and all claims, damages, losses or other liability (including attorneys' fees and costs) arising from any breach of this covenant.

## Editorial note on cross-references

The recorded document contains several internal cross-references that do not match the section numbering of the recorded instrument. These references are reproduced above exactly as recorded and have not been corrected. The Neighborhood used a systematic section scheme in which many provisions reference a number one lower than the section that actually contains the referenced material, apparently the result of renumbering that was not carried through to the internal references. The discrepancies are:

- Section 2.8 (Lease and Rent Restrictions) states that certain agreements "violate this Section 2.7." Section 2.7 of the recorded Declaration is "Single Family Residence." In context the reference reads as Section 2.8.
- Section 2.14.3 refers to a use "allowed in Section 2.13.3(a) below." Section 2.13 is "Holiday Decorations." In context the reference reads as Section 2.14.3(a).
- Section 2.14.3(a)(vi) refers twice to "this Section 2.13" (a vehicle "in violation of this Section 2.13" and "verification of adherence to this Section 2.13"). In context both read as Section 2.14.
- Section 2.14.6 (Enforcement) directs the Board to "enforce the provisions of this Section 2.13." In context the reference reads as Section 2.14.
- Section 2.16.4 (Review after Installation) refers to building-exterior review "as described in Section 2.15.3 above." Section 2.15 is "Animal Regulations." In context the reference reads as Section 2.16.3.
- Section 2.17 (Trash) states that "Failure to strictly abide by this Section 2.16" may result in disciplinary action. Section 2.16 is "Antenna Restrictions." In context the reference reads as Section 2.17.
- Section 2.18 (Installations) opens "Except for subsection 2.17.4, this Section 2.17 does not apply." Section 2.17 is "Trash." In context these references read as subsection 2.18.4 and Section 2.18.
