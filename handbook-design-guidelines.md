# Design Guidelines

This page reproduces the Design Guidelines standards, including lighting, window coverings, screen doors, balconies, hard surface flooring, sound attenuation, signs, waterbeds and aquariums, sports equipment, and the Satellite Dish and Antenna Policy.

Source: Neighborhood Guidelines (Residential Handbook), Tribeca at Central Park West Association, adopted by the Board July 24, 2018.

---

## Introduction to the Design Guidelines

These Design Guidelines are designed with the goal of maintaining the aesthetic beauty, and preserving the safety, value and desirability of living at Tribeca. By adhering to these Design Guidelines, which include guidelines and standards for all improvements and sound and noise guidelines, all Owners will benefit from the beauty and enjoyment of Tribeca.

No Owner or resident may remove or otherwise alter any common wall separating Units so as to merge or otherwise unite two (2) or more Units.

No Owner shall drill, penetrate or otherwise tamper with the concrete or other structural components of Tribeca, including the Exclusive Use Areas.

All work must be done by a license contractor.

Prior to making any improvements to your Unit, including any Exclusive Use Area balcony, you must first submit a complete application to the Design Review Committee ("DRC"). After receiving written approval from the DRC and complying with applicable city/governmental agencies, you may install your improvements, or undertake your approved action. Please review these Design Guidelines prior to completing your application form to ensure your submittal is complete. In the event of a conflict between these Design Guidelines and the Neighborhood Declaration, the Neighborhood Declaration shall prevail. In the event of conflict between the Neighborhood Declaration and the Master Declaration, the Master Declaration shall prevail.

It is recommended that you also refer to Article 5 of the Neighborhood Declaration in conjunction with these Design Guidelines to ensure a complete understanding of the submittal and review process. If at any time you have any questions regarding the review process, please contact the Manager.

## Design Guidelines

These Design Guidelines are not intended to restrict individual creativity or personal preference, but rather to assure and preserve the value, desirability, attractiveness and Design integrity of Tribeca.

The standards set forth below shall apply to the improvements within the Neighborhood. These standards are in addition to the standards set forth in the Rules and Regulations and the other Governing Documents.

**Lighting Exterior:** No exterior electrical, gas or other artificial exterior lighting shall be installed other than lighting initially installed by the Builder or temporary holiday lighting.

**Window Coverings and Treatment:** Subject to extension by the Board, Owners may cover their windows with white sheets for up to three (3) months prior to installing permanent window coverings. Permanent window coverings must have either a white or cream color backing or may be white or light-colored natural wood shutters. All window coverings shall be of a uniform neutral color harmonious with and not in conflict with the color scheme of the exterior wall surface of the building. Window tinting is not permitted.

**Screen Doors:** Only retractable screen doors are permitted and do not require DRC review as long as they are harmonious with the trim color of the home.

### Exclusive Use Area Balconies

**1. Outdoor Furniture:** Balcony furniture and other similar outdoor furnishings must be in good condition. These furnishings must be equipped with protective leg caps or other devices to prevent damage to the floor of the Exclusive Use balcony. Additionally, none of these furnishings or other improvement shall be nailed, bolted, or otherwise attached to the floor, walls, or any other portion of the Exclusive Use Area balcony.

**2. Plants:** No personal plants can be added to any Association Area Property, such as the plantings near a Unit entry. Potted plant vegetation that extends beyond the railings, fences, walls and/or other boundaries of an Exclusive Use Areas is prohibited. Saucers to catch the water must be placed under all potted plants to protect the Exclusive Use Area balcony flooring. Live plants are not allowed on small balconies with no access, on pot shelves or on wall caps. All other items requiring water may not be kept in any Exclusive Use Area balconies in the Neighborhood.

**3. Speakers:** Installation of any outdoor speakers is prohibited.

### Hard Surface Flooring

**1.** No hard surface flooring can be installed in the restricted areas shown on Exhibit "D" to the Neighborhood Declaration. Owners may replace hard surface flooring installed by Declarant, provided the Owner replaces the flooring with materials that are the same as or substantially similar to the original materials installed by the Declarant (including underlayment as applicable).

**2.** If an Owner installs hard surface flooring in any portion of the Unit without installing materials which are the same as or substantially similar to the original materials installed by the Declarant, the Neighborhood Association may require the Owner of such Unit to remove the flooring at the Owner's sole cost and expense and re-install the flooring with materials which are the same as or substantially similar to the original materials installed by the Declarant.

**3.** Each Owner must ensure that any changes to the Unit (especially plumbing and flooring) shall not have the effect of increasing the level of noise or sounds that can be heard outside of the Unit above the sound level (impact and airborne sounds) of 55 dB(A).

**4.** All changes to floors separating Units (tile, hardwood, stone, carpet, etc.) must provide code-compliant sound control properties for airborne and impact sound insulation. In addition, the floor/ceiling assemblies must satisfy the higher sound control requirements established for the Neighborhood as set forth herein. The impact sound insulation rating of the floor ceiling assemblies after installation must be Field Impact Insulation Class ("FIIC") 50 or higher. Airborne sound isolation rating thereof must be Noise Isolation Class ("NIC") 52 or higher.

**5.** As a condition to approving the installation or replacement of hard surface flooring, the Owner shall submit to the DRC a construction drawing and specifications clearly indicating the type of flooring to be installed and the underlayment material to be provided to mitigate against impact noises such as footfalls and transmission of music and other sounds. The submission must clearly identify all materials, their composition and thickness, and include a report by an accredited acoustical testing laboratory showing that a test specimen essentially identical to the proposed assembly achieved a Sound Transmission Class ("STC") rating of not less than STC-54 and an Impact Insulation Class ("IIC") rating of not less than IIC-52. Following approval and installation of the new flooring assembly, a field test conducted under the supervision of a person experienced in the field of acoustical testing shall be performed in each room in which the flooring is installed to determine compliance with the requirements of NIC 52 or higher and FIIC 50 or higher. The required field test shall be completed within thirty (30) days after substantial completion of the work, subject to any extensions granted by the Committee or the Board. Non-compliant systems must be promptly brought into conformance or removed and replaced with approved materials/assemblies.

### Sound Attenuation

In any multi-family dwelling, sound may be audible between Units, particularly where the sound level of the source is sufficiently high and the background noise in an adjacent Unit is very low. Each Owner shall endeavor to minimize any noise transmission from his or her Unit.

**1.** No holes or other penetrations shall be made in Party Walls without the prior approval of the DRC. No penetrations of any sort shall be made in the ceiling of any Unit or any floor. Acoustical sealant shall be packed around all holes made by nails or screws when hanging items from the wall.

**2.** No modifications shall be made to any Unit which would result in a reduction in the minimum impact insulation class of the Unit.

**3.** Speakers for music reproduction, television and other audio-visual devices shall not be supported from or in contact with Party Walls and shall be elevated from the floor by a proper acoustic platform.

**4.** Pianos shall have at least one-half inch (1/2") neoprene pads under the supports to minimize vibration transmission into the structure.

**5.** All alterations to walls and floors must be field tested under the supervision of a person experienced in the field of acoustical testing to determine compliance with the minimum sound control standards at the Unit Owners sole cost and expense. Non-compliant systems must be promptly brought into conformance or removed and replaced with approved materials/assemblies.

### Signs

Subject to Civil Code Sections 712, 713 and 4710, no sign, advertising device or other display of any kind shall be displayed in the Neighborhood Association Property or on any public street in or abutting the Neighborhood except for the following:

**1.** Each Unit may have no more than one (1) for sale or lease sign that is no larger than eighteen inches (18") by twenty-four inches (24") in size, and shall be of a color and style authorized by the Board. The sign may only be posted in the area designated for such purpose by the Neighborhood Association.

**2.** One (1) nameplate or similar Owner name or address identification.

**3.** Non-commercial signs permitted by law, poster, flag, banner displayed only from a window, door balcony or other area within exclusive control of the Owner. The Neighborhood Association may prohibit noncommercial signs and poster that are more than nine (9) square feet in size and noncommercial flags or banners that are more than fifteen (15) square feet in size.

**4.** Any other sign or display authorized by the DRC, the Board, or otherwise allowed by law.

**5.** No sign advertising the Unit for sale or lease may be placed on Association Property. The preferred location for such sign is in a window.

### Waterbeds and Aquariums

Waterbeds are not permitted. Aquariums are limited to a container holding 30 gallons or less, unless the DRC committee approves a larger container.

### Sports and Other Equipment

No exterior roof mounted mechanical equipment, poles or masts shall be constructed on or attached to or erected or maintained on any Unit or Exclusive Use area. No temporary or permanent basketball standard or backboard, or other sports apparatus shall be constructed, erected, installed or maintained on any Unit, Exclusive Use area, or on any Neighborhood Association Property in Tribeca.

## Satellite Dish and Antenna Policy

**Antennas:** No Person may install on the exterior of any Unit or balcony railings any antenna or over-the-air receiving device except for an "Authorized Antenna." An Authorized Antenna is (a) an antenna designed to receive direct broadcast satellite service, including direct-to-home satellite service, that is one (1) meter or less in diameter, (b) an antenna designed to receive video programming service, including multi-channel multipoint distribution service, instructional television fixed service, and local multipoint distribution service, and is one (1) meter or less in diameter or diagonal measurement, (c) an antenna designed to receive television broadcast signals, or (d) an antenna used to receive and transmit fixed wireless signals or broadband service. An Authorized Antenna may only be mounted on a tripod, which may not be fastened to a Building surface such as to cause damage thereto, to reach the height needed to receive an acceptable quality signal, subject to local governmental agency permitting requirements for safety purposes.

No Owner shall install any antenna or other Improvement on a Building exterior or on any real property which such Owner does not own, such as the walls enclosing the Owner's Exclusive Use Area balcony or balcony railings, or is not entitled to exclusively use or control under the Governing Documents. No restriction imposed by the Committee may (a) unreasonably delay or prevent the installation, maintenance or use of an Authorized Antenna, (b) unreasonably increase the cost of the installation, maintenance or use of an Authorized Antenna, or (c) preclude acceptable quality reception.

### 1. Areas Under an Owner's Exclusive Use and Control

**(a) Satellite Dishes.** Satellite dishes may be installed in an area under a Owner's exclusive use or control so long as such satellite dishes are installed in the least visually obtrusive portion of a Owner's Exclusive Use Area balcony on a tripod where an acceptable quality signal can be received, either screened from view or painted to match the surrounding area so as to blend in with the surrounding area, and must not be attached to any portion of the building, Exclusive Use Area balcony or balcony railing, or glass doors. Owners shall be responsible for ensuring that the dish stand is weighed down so that the dish is not blown over the balcony railing.

**(b) Request for Installation.** Owners must complete and submit a Request for Installation of Satellite Dish or Antenna form to the Neighborhood Association prior to any installations. Once approved by the Neighborhood Association and installed, the Neighborhood Association will inspect the satellite dish or antenna to determine compliance with the requirements. After its review, the Neighborhood Association may require that the Authorized Antenna be moved for safety reasons or to comply with reasonable restrictions subject to the Neighborhood Declaration and applicable law. No wiring may penetrate any portion of a building in any way, nor use down-spouts or gutters, or run on or along the building exterior.

### 2. Areas Not Under Owner's Exclusive Use or Control

Owners are not permitted to install satellite dishes and antennae in areas that are not under the Owner's exclusive use or control. Such areas include Neighborhood Association Property, including but not limited to, landscaped areas; Exclusive Use Area balcony railings, walls or ceilings; the roof and the building exteriors. Owners must immediately remove and repair any holes or other damage to Neighborhood Association Property or the Exclusive Use Area balcony if the Neighborhood Association requires the Owner to remove or relocate the antenna or satellite dish.
